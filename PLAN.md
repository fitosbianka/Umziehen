# The Moving Plan

Noah has applied for the four year KFO specialization in Bern, Zürich and Basel. Until one of them says yes, the honest answer to "where will we live" is a plan with three branches. This document is that plan. The interactive map that belongs to it lives here: https://claude.ai/artifact/3APp6rKyhSLPgrPFYcBeeF

The three clinics, confirmed:

* **Bern**: ZMK Bern, Klinik für Kieferorthopädie, Freiburgstrasse 7, 3010 Bern, directly next to the Inselspital. From Bern main station bus 12 reaches it in a few minutes.
* **Zürich**: ZZM, Klinik für Kieferorthopädie und Kinderzahnmedizin, Plattenstrasse 11, 8032 Zürich, up at the Platte tram stop about ten minutes above the main station.
* **Basel**: UZB, Universitäres Zentrum für Zahnmedizin Basel, Mattenstrasse 40, 4058 Basel, by the Messe, a short tram from both Basel stations.

The specialization runs four years, full time, five clinic days a week. That number shapes everything below.

## What is true in every scenario

* You are in Kehrsatz four days a week, whatever happens. Your ride has to stay humane for four years, not four weeks.
* Noah is at his clinic five days a week. House rule: the five day person gets the shorter commute.
* No car. Every address must work by train, tram, bus and Velo, in November as well as June.
* The parents in Kehrsatz and Niederscherli are worth real minutes on the map. The day a baby arrives, a grandmother twenty minutes away outweighs a prettier balcony.
* Budget 2000 to 2500 for 3.5 to 4.5 rooms. That works everywhere in this plan except central Zürich.
* Calm street, no exceptions. Wabern taught you what a main road does to a home. Before any viewing, check the address on the federal noise map (map.geo.admin.ch, search for road noise) and visit at evening rush.
* Your notice period is presumably three months to an official term. Nothing gets cancelled until an acceptance is in writing, so there is no rush and no risk right now, only preparation.

## How we decide

A surprising fact from the numbers: once you leave the Bern belt, the total commuting load of the household lands between about 15 and 18 hours a week in nearly every reasonable setup. What changes is who carries it and what you get in return. So the decision is never "which place has the least commuting". It is: who carries the hours, how much money is left, how far the grandparents are, and whether the place feels like home. That is the lens for all three branches.

## Scenario 1: Bern says yes

The easy yes. Work, parents, budget and family plans all point the same way. The only real task is to finally get the quiet street.

Where to look, in order:

* **Kehrsatz**, the top pick. Your commute becomes a short walk, one set of parents lives in the village, Noah takes the S3 or S31 and is at the ZMK in about 35 minutes. 4.5 rooms around 1800 to 2350. Away from the main road it is properly calm.
* **Spiegel**, the other top pick. Leafy family streets on the sunny slope between the city and Kehrsatz. You walk down to the S3 at Kehrsatz Nord, Noah takes bus 19. Few flats come up, so the alert matters.
* **Wabern, quiet side**. Everything you already love, minus the Seftigenstrasse. Two streets toward Eichholz and the Gurten the noise stops.
* **Weissenbühl** if you want more city, **Liebefeld or Köniz and Schliern** if you want young families and the S6 that also reaches Niederscherli, **Belp** for the most space per franc on your own S3, **Niederscherli** if grandparent support should be next door.
* **Fribourg** as the wildcard of the heart: both of you become daily commuters, about an hour for you, 45 minutes for Noah. Ride it once on a grey Tuesday before deciding.

Money: about 7000 from you plus about 3000 from Noah. Hold the rent nearer 2000 to 2200 during the four lean years and save the rest. Only one modest travel pass is needed. Later, the Bern region childcare vouchers are one more reason this scenario is the family scenario.

Steps once the yes arrives: give notice for the next official term, aim for one overlap month, apply with the ready dossier on day one for Kehrsatz and Spiegel listings, and think one chapter ahead: a flat that fits a crib in year two saves a second move at the worst moment.

## Scenario 2: Zürich says yes

Move to the middle, not to Zürich. With your four fixed Kehrsatz days, a flat in Zürich means about 100 minutes each way for you. A midpoint town keeps both of you under about 70 minutes, and Zürich pay makes this the richest of the three futures: about 14000 joint.

Three models, decide the model before the address:

1. **Midpoint town**, recommended. **Aarau** is the midpoint with the most life: a genuinely lovely old town, calm family quarters like Gönhard and Zelgli, about 50 minutes for Noah to the ZZM and about 70 for you to Kehrsatz. **Olten** is the value twin: about 52 minutes for each of you, the lowest rents of the whole plan, and trains in every direction every few minutes. Aarau for charm, Olten for money and symmetry.
2. **City flat**, only if your Kehrsatz days can bend. Wollishofen is calm and lakeside but stretches the budget past 2500. Affoltern is the budget door into the city. Both mean 100 minute rides for you, four days a week.
3. **Weekday room**. Keep a Bern belt flat and rent Noah a room near the ZZM for 800 to 1000 a month. Home every weekend. Cheapest in life disruption for you, hardest on the relationship. Couples do survive four years of this in Switzerland, but choose it together and honestly, and revisit it the moment a baby is on the way.

Also on the map: **Burgdorf**, the Zähringer castle town with a small Fribourg feeling, 45 minutes for you and close to the parents, but about 1h20 for Noah five days a week. Only fair if he genuinely prefers a Bern base over sleep.

Money: two GA passes at about 4000 each per year are simply part of this scenario. Even so, it leaves the biggest surplus by far. Rent, do not buy: the likely landing after the four years is back toward Bern, your clinic and the grandparents, with Noah as a Fachzahnarzt looking at practices.

## Scenario 3: Basel says yes

Olten splits this one almost perfectly. If you would rather live the city years in Basel itself, stay on the station side so your Bern train starts with a short walk, not a tram odyssey.

Where to look, in order:

* **Olten**, top pick. 26 minutes to Bern, 26 minutes to Basel, from the same platform. About 52 minutes door to door for you, about 50 for Noah. Also the one address that survives a Zürich yes instead, which makes it the hedge of the whole plan.
* **Basel Gundeldingen**, the city pick. Eight minutes on foot to the station for your train days, then about 1h25 in total. Noah trams across town in under half an hour. Quiet grid streets toward Margarethen, markets, young families. 4.5 rooms touch the top of the budget.
* **Basel Hirzbrunnen** if Noah walking to work is worth your longer ride, **Riehen** as the family village for a later chapter, **Liestal** as the practical value option on the Basel side.
* **Solothurn** as the wildcard: the most Fribourg feeling outside Fribourg, a baroque old town that counts everything in elevens, direct trains to Bern. But Noah changes in Olten five days a week, about 70 minutes each way.

Money: your 7000 plus a Basel salary that is still unknown. Ask the UZB about the assistant salary in the very first conversation, and plan with 3000 to 4000 until they confirm. In Olten or Liestal the budget is easy either way. In Basel city hold the line around 2300 until the number is real. If you live in Basel city, one GA for you may be all you need.

## The Fribourg feeling

You asked for places that feel like Fribourg. Ranked by resemblance: **Solothurn** first, then **Aarau**, then **Burgdorf**. All three are old towns on a river with real life in them. Fribourg itself only fits the Bern scenario, where it is the honest wildcard: cheaper rents and the city you love, paid for in daily train time and distance to the parents.

## Money at a glance

* **Bern**: about 10000 joint. Rent 2000 to 2400 is comfortable. Smallest commuting cost in francs and hours. The family scenario.
* **Zürich**: about 14000 joint, minus about 8000 a year for two GA passes. Still the richest future. The money scenario.
* **Basel**: 7000 plus an unknown. Plan conservatively, decide city versus midpoint once the UZB confirms. The open scenario.

## Timeline and checklist

This week, before any letter:

* Set search alerts on Flatfox, Homegate and Newhome for all three scenarios: Kehrsatz, Spiegel, Wabern, Liebefeld, Köniz for Bern. Aarau and Olten for Zürich. Olten, Gundeldingen, Liestal for Basel. 3.5 to 4.5 rooms, up to 2500. Deleting an alert later costs nothing.
* Build the application dossier once, use it three times: employment confirmations for both of you, ID copies, and order a fresh Betreibungsregisterauszug only when you actually apply, it should be under three months old.
* Read your current lease: notice period, official term dates, and whether subletting would be allowed as a bridge.

The day an acceptance arrives:

* Choose the model first (this document), the address second, the flat third.
* Give notice in time for the next official term, aim for one overlap month.
* Spend one Saturday walking the shortlisted quarter, plus one rush hour visit for any flat you want.
* You both test your real commute once before signing, out the door at working time, not on a Sunday.
* If timing gaps open, bridge with a furnished temporary flat rather than panic signing a loud one. You already paid that lesson once on the Seftigenstrasse.

One last rule, written down so future you can quote it: the five clinic days get the short ride, nobody signs on a main road, and every franc of rent below 2400 in the lean years is a franc toward the family chapter.
